Harbour Lane Studio - Staff Handbook

About the studio

Harbour Lane Studio is a product design and software studio founded in 2014 in Nelson. We build booking, logistics and field-service software for mid-sized businesses across New Zealand and Australia. The studio has 42 staff across two offices. The Nelson office on Trafalgar Street is the head office and holds most of the design, engineering and operations teams. The Wellington office on Cuba Street has 11 people, mostly account managers and two engineering squads who work closely with our largest government client. This handbook explains how we work together, what the studio provides, and what we expect in return. It is updated every January by the operations team, and any change made during the year is announced in the Monday all-hands meeting and posted in the handbook channel. If something in this handbook conflicts with your employment agreement, the employment agreement wins. If something is unclear, ask your team lead first and the operations team second. Nobody will think less of you for asking; most of the rules below exist because someone asked a good question once.

Working hours and remote work

Our working week is 40 hours, and we care much more about outcomes than about when you are at your desk. The one fixed commitment is core hours: everyone should be reachable between 10:00 and 15:00 on weekdays, New Zealand time, so that meetings, reviews and quick questions have a reliable window. Outside core hours you can arrange your day around school runs, training or whatever else your life needs, as long as your team knows your pattern. You may work from home up to three days a week once you have finished probation. Most teams choose Tuesday and Wednesday as their shared office days, so that in-person time is actually spent together rather than on video calls from neighbouring desks. Fridays are meeting-free across the studio. Use them for deep work, learning, or tidying up the week. If you need to work fully remotely for a period, for example while caring for a family member, talk to your team lead; we have approved arrangements of up to three months without fuss.

Leave

Every permanent staff member receives 25 days of annual leave per year, which is five days more than the legal minimum. Leave accrues from your first day and can be used as soon as it has accrued. You can carry over up to five days of unused annual leave into the next year; anything above that is paid out in the April pay run. For leave longer than three consecutive working days, please request it at least two weeks ahead in the HR system so that your team can plan cover. Shorter leave only needs a heads-up to your team lead. Sick leave is ten days per year, and you do not need a medical certificate unless you are away for more than three consecutive days. Sick leave also covers caring for a sick dependant. For parental leave, the studio tops up the government payment to your full salary for the first 12 weeks, for any parent, whether birth, adoptive or whangai. Bereavement leave is five days for the loss of a close family member and one day for others who were important to you, and team leads have discretion to give more.

Expenses

The studio reimburses reasonable costs you incur while working for us. Submit every expense claim in the Tally app within 30 days of spending the money; claims older than that will be declined unless your team lead explains the delay. Attach a receipt for anything over $50. A photo of the receipt taken in Tally is enough, and you do not need to keep the paper copy. Any single purchase above $500 needs approval from your team lead before you buy it, not after. Purchases above $5,000 also need sign-off from the operations manager. When travelling within New Zealand for work, meals are covered up to $65 per day, and alcohol is not reimbursed except at client events approved in advance. Reimbursements are paid in the next weekly pay run after approval. If you are regularly spending your own money on studio costs, ask operations for a studio card instead so that you are not out of pocket.

Travel

Book all work travel through Ember Travel, our travel agent, using the booking link in the travel channel. Booking through Ember means the studio pays directly, the trip is covered by our travel insurance, and operations can find you in an emergency. Flights under five hours are economy class. For longer flights, premium economy is allowed if you are working on the day you land. Choose accommodation within walking distance of where you will be working, up to the nightly limit shown in Ember. Use public transport, taxis or ride-share at your destination; hire a rental car only if there is no reasonable public transport option. If you use your own car for work trips, the studio pays a mileage allowance of 99 cents per kilometre, claimed through Tally with the start and end addresses. Travel time during a normal working day counts as work. If a trip forces you to travel on a weekend, you can take the equivalent time off in the following two weeks.

Equipment and IT

Every staff member gets a laptop on their first day, set up by the IT team with our standard software and security tools. Laptops are replaced every three years, or sooner if yours can no longer run the tools your role needs. You also receive a one-off home office allowance of $750 in your first three months, to spend on a desk, chair, monitor or anything else that makes working at home comfortable; claim it through Tally like any other expense. The equipment you buy with it is yours to keep. Use the studio password manager for every work account. Passwords must be at least 16 characters, and multi-factor authentication is required on email, source control and the cloud consoles. If you lose a laptop or work phone, or think it has been stolen, report it to the IT team within one hour by messaging the IT channel or calling the on-call number on the back of your access card. We would much rather wipe a device that turns up later than miss the window to protect client data.

Security and client data

Much of our work touches client data, so security is part of everyone's job, not just the IT team's. Classify documents using the three labels in the drive: Public, Internal and Restricted. Restricted documents, which include anything containing client customer records, must never be downloaded to personal devices or shared outside the client workspace. Visitors to either office sign in at reception, wear a visitor badge, and are accompanied at all times outside the kitchen and meeting rooms. Keep your desk clear of printed client material at the end of each day. If an email looks suspicious, use the report button in the mail client rather than forwarding it; the IT team reviews every report within the working day and will tell you what they found. Nobody at the studio will ever ask you for your password, including the IT team. If you think you have made a mistake, such as clicking a link or sending a file to the wrong person, tell IT straight away. Reporting quickly is always the right call and nobody gets in trouble for it.

Onboarding and probation

Your first week is mostly about meeting people and getting set up rather than delivering work. On day one you are assigned an onboarding buddy from outside your own team, who will check in with you daily for the first two weeks and answer the questions you do not want to ask your team lead. By the end of your first week you should have shipped one small change, written or visual, so that you have seen our full review and release process. All new permanent staff start with a probation period of 90 days. Your team lead will hold a check-in at six weeks to talk through what is going well and anything that needs to change, and a final review before the 90 days end. Probation is about fit in both directions, and you are encouraged to raise anything that is not working for you. Remote work of up to three days a week starts once probation ends, although team leads can agree to it earlier.

Professional development

We want everyone to leave the studio more skilled than they arrived. Each staff member has a learning budget of $1,800 per year for courses, books, certifications and conference tickets, and five paid study days to use alongside it. The budget resets on the first of April and does not carry over. You may attend one conference per year on studio time, and the studio covers travel costs for it through Ember in addition to the learning budget. If you attend, we ask you to share what you learned in a short talk at the Friday lunch session within a month of getting back. Internal mentoring is also available: any staff member can ask the operations team to pair them with a mentor from another team for a six-month period. Study days can be split into half days. Please book them in the HR system like leave so that your team knows when you are unavailable.

Health and safety

Both offices have a trained first aider on every floor, listed on the noticeboard by the kitchen, and a first aid kit in each kitchen. The fire warden for the Nelson office is Priya Raman on level two, and for the Wellington office it is Tom Kereama. When the fire alarm sounds, leave by the nearest stairwell, never the lift, and go to the assembly point. The Nelson assembly point is Anzac Park, across the road from the main entrance; the Wellington assembly point is the corner of Cuba and Ghuznee Streets. In an earthquake, drop, cover and hold until the shaking stops, then wait for instructions from the fire warden before leaving the building. Report every injury or near miss in the safety form, even minor ones, within 24 hours. The studio pays for an annual flu vaccination and an eye test every two years for anyone who spends most of the day at a screen.

Pay and benefits

Salaries are paid fortnightly on a Wednesday, directly into the bank account you give the operations team on your first day. Payslips are available in the HR system from the Monday before each pay day. Salaries are reviewed once a year in July, using market data for each role and the feedback from your two performance conversations. We publish salary bands for every role internally, so you can see where you sit and what the next band looks like. After three months with the studio you are eligible for health insurance, paid in full by the studio for you and at a subsidised rate for your partner and children. The studio also provides income protection insurance that covers 75 percent of your salary for up to two years if you cannot work because of illness or injury. Every quarter, each staff member receives a wellbeing allowance of $200 that can be spent on a gym membership, sports gear, a massage, or anything else that helps you stay well; claim it through Tally with a receipt. Staff who refer a candidate who is hired and passes probation receive a referral bonus of $2,000.

Performance and feedback

We hold two structured performance conversations a year, in March and September, between you and your team lead. Each one looks back at the last six months and agrees on two or three goals for the next six. Before each conversation you will be asked for peer feedback on four to six colleagues, and they will be asked about you; keep it specific and kind. Promotions are considered in the September round, based on the expectations written down for each level in the career framework. Nobody should be surprised by anything said in a review. If there is a problem, your team lead should raise it when it happens, not save it up. Equally, if you are unhappy with how you are being managed, you can talk to the operations manager or to any other team lead in confidence. Weekly one-to-ones of at least 30 minutes are expected for every team, and you are welcome to set the agenda for them.

How we communicate

Most day-to-day conversation happens in the team chat. Use public channels by default, so that others can learn from the discussion and nobody becomes a single point of knowledge, and keep direct messages for personal or sensitive topics. Email is mainly for clients and suppliers; reply to client emails within one working day, even if the reply is only to say when you will have a full answer. Decisions that affect more than one team are written up as a short proposal in the shared drive and discussed at the Thursday leads meeting. The Monday all-hands meeting runs for 30 minutes at 9:30 and is recorded for anyone who cannot attend. Set your chat status when you are on leave, at a client site, or doing focused work, and turn notifications off outside your working hours. You are never expected to reply to messages in the evening or at the weekend unless you are on the incident rota.

Client work

Every client project starts with a statement of work that sets out scope, budget and timeline, signed by the client before any work begins. Record your time in Harvest against the right project each day, and complete your timesheet by 16:00 every Friday, because client invoices are generated from it on Monday morning. The billable target for delivery roles is 70 percent of working hours; time spent on internal projects, hiring and learning is tracked separately and is just as valued. Scope changes requested by a client must go through a written change request, approved by the account manager, before the work starts. Never promise a client a date that your team lead has not agreed to. Client meetings are attended by at least two people from the studio, so that knowledge is shared and nobody is left holding a relationship alone. Project retrospectives are held at the end of every project and every three months on long-running work.

Code review and releases

Every change to client software is reviewed before it is merged. Changes need two approvals, at least one from an engineer outside the pair that wrote the change. Automated tests and security checks must pass before a change can be merged, and nobody may override a failing check without the tech lead's written approval. We release to production on Tuesdays and Thursdays, after the morning stand-up. There are no releases after 12:00 on a Friday, or in the two days before a public holiday, except for urgent security fixes. Each engineer joins the incident on-call rota roughly one week in six, and receives an on-call allowance of $300 for each week on the rota, plus time off in lieu for any call-out that happens at night. After every serious incident we write a blameless review within five working days, focused on what the system allowed to happen rather than on who made the mistake.

Social and wellbeing

The social club organises something roughly every month, from quiz nights to a winter bonfire, and the studio covers the cost of one team day per quarter. On Fridays the studio buys lunch for each office, and the lunch session is where people share talks, demos and conference notes. The studio pays for the Employee Assistance Programme, which offers six free, confidential counselling sessions a year to you and your immediate family, on anything from work stress to money worries. Nobody at the studio is told who uses it. Quiet rooms are available in both offices for prayer, rest or a phone call that needs privacy; book them in the calendar like a meeting room. If you want to start a club or interest group, the social club will fund the first gathering.

Leaving the studio

If you decide to leave, the notice period for permanent staff is four weeks, unless your employment agreement says otherwise. Tell your team lead first, and then confirm in writing to the operations team. Return your laptop, access card and any other studio equipment within five working days of your last day; equipment bought with the home office allowance is yours to keep. Any remaining annual leave is paid out in your final pay. We will ask you to take part in a short exit interview with someone outside your team, so that we can learn from what worked and what did not. Former staff are welcome at the end-of-year party, and many people have come back to the studio after working elsewhere.
